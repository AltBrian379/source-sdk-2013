OWTF2 - Crusader (Reinhardt-like tank hero): plan (DRAFT)
=========================================================

Gameplay of Overwatch's Reinhardt, recreated with an original name. Nothing from Blizzard
(names, models, sounds, art) goes into the mod. This is a first draft: the kit below is what
the user described; more cases come later, and assets get their own section once described.

Naming
------
- Display name: "Crusader". (TF2 already has the Crusader's Crossbow, and the Combat Medic
  uses its model as a placeholder; only the display text could be confused, the code can't.)
- Code: TF_CLASS_CRUSADER (12, after Combat Medic; Civilian moves to 13, Random to 15).
  Raw name "crusader", class script scripts/playerclasses/crusader.txt.
- Functions Crusader_*, classes CTFCrusader*, convars tf_crusader_* (FCVAR_REPLICATED |
  FCVAR_NOTIFY). Every tunable number is a convar, commented with its Overwatch source value.

Placeholders and stats
----------------------
- Model: the Demoman (the Targe's charge animations and the Eyelander's swings exist for him).
  Chosen by the user for phase 0.
- Base speed 280, like every ported hero.
- Health: 385 (the user's value; crusader.txt). Hammer damage 70 (the user's value): a
  Crusader takes 6 swings (385 / 70 = 5.5).
- Damage scale: TF2 health / Overwatch health. Reinhardt's Overwatch health has changed often
  (5v5 vs 6v6 patches, health/armor splits); check the live value before Phase 0 and compute
  the scale then. Check the mirror time to kill (hammer swings to kill a Crusader).
- TODO: armor health, as in Overwatch. Reinhardt's health is part health, part armor, and
  armor reduces the damage of each hit that lands on it. TF2 has no armor, so it's a new
  shared system (any hero can use it): an armor pool on the player, used up before health, with
  its own per-hit damage reduction, plus a HUD display. How much of the 300 is armor, the
  reduction rule, and how healing refills it are decided when it's built (check the live
  Overwatch rules then, not now).
- Overwatch values to check before each phase (sources disagree; none confirmed yet):
  hammer damage and swing time, Fire Strike damage, cooldown and charges, Charge cooldown,
  duration, speed and pin damage, Earthshatter range, cone width and stun time.

Loadout and keys (proposal)
---------------------------
  Slot 1  Rocket hammer   +attack swings. The only real weapon (melee as the main weapon).
  -       Charge          +attack4 (SHIFT), the class ability key.
  -       Fire Strike     needs a key. Overwatch uses E; the mod has no E ability key yet.
                          Options: a new +attack6 bound to E, or MOUSE2 until a barrier
                          exists. TO DECIDE.
  -       Ultimate        +attack5 (Q), the ultimate system ("Earthshatter"-like; name TBD).
Fire Strike is a cast (the hammer can't swing during it): a CTFCastAbilityWeapon, or a cast
time on the hammer like the Veteran's helix (m_flHelixCastEndTime pattern). The second avoids a
weapon switch away from the hammer; preferred.

Hammer swing (Phase 2)
----------------------
- Already in (ahead of phase 2, for the animations): tf_weapon_crusader_hammer
  (CTFCrusaderHammer, TF_WEAPON_CRUSADER_HAMMER), item 60005, the primary on key 1, a melee
  weapon. 70 damage (the user's value). PLACEHOLDER: the Eyelander's model and sounds.
- Swing timing (DONE, the user's Overwatch values): 1.05 s per swing (TimeFireDelay), split into
  cast tf_crusader_hammer_cast_time (0.4 s, no hitbox), active tf_crusader_hammer_sweep_time
  (0.21 s: the 10 rays, one every 0.021 s) and recovery (the rest, 0.44 s). The user's recovery
  was 0.5 s, which with 0.4 + 0.21 adds up to 1.11, not 1.05: the 1.05 fire rate was kept.
  Tested: swings 1.05 s apart, every hit 0.40-0.54 s into its swing.
- Viewmodel timing (DONE): the swing sequence's playback rate changes per phase
  (UpdateSwingAnimRate, predicted): its wind-up fills the cast, the frames where the blade
  crosses the screen play over the active time, the rest fills the recovery. Crossing frames
  (measured frame by frame in slow motion; cycles of the 1.0 s sequences): cm_swing_a
  0.19-0.33, cm_swing_b 0.23-0.37. Rates: left 0.475 / 0.667 / 1.52, right 0.575 / 0.667 /
  1.43. Tested: the cycle is 0.19 at 0.40 s, 0.33 at 0.61 s and 0.99 at 1.05 s; screenshots
  show the blade crossing between 0.41 and 0.56 s. No prediction errors.
- Knockback (DONE): TF2's damage-based push (ApplyPushFromDamage: DamageForce(hull, damage,
  tf_damageforcescale_other 6), half on Heavies, none on buildings), but straight sideways in
  the swing's direction (the camera's left for a left swing; horizontal; no push toward or away
  from the Crusader). CTFPlayer::AdjustDamageDirection (OWTF2 override) asks the weapon
  (AlternatingSwing_AdjustDamageDirection) while a sweep hit is applied. Tested on standing
  Soldier bots: 70 damage pushes 420 HU/s to the left on a left swing, to the right on a right
  swing, about 0 forward.
- Hit (DONE, the user's design; replaces TF2's one trace and the all-at-once arc idea above): a sweep of
  rays (CTFAlternatingSwingMelee's sweep, GetSweepParams). tf_crusader_hammer_rays (10) rays
  across tf_crusader_hammer_arc (65 degrees) in front of the camera, tf_crusader_hammer_range
  (200 HU: 5 m = 197) long, fired one at a time evenly through the active time (see "Swing
  timing": ray i at 0.4 + i * 0.021 s),
  in the swing's direction (a left swing sweeps from the right edge to the left edge). Each ray
  is aimed from the camera when it fires: turning while swinging widens the arc. The world and
  props stop a ray; every enemy player or building whose hull it crosses is hit; the space
  between consecutive rays is filled (extra rays at most 1 degree apart). A target is hit once
  per swing. Server side, lag compensated; TF2's hit handling (OnSwingHit: sounds, damage,
  crits). Debug: tf_alternating_swing_debug 1 (cheat) draws the rays.
  Tested (itemtest, standing Heavy bots, random crits off): enemies at 25 degrees right / ahead
  / 25 degrees left, 100-180 HU: left swing hit them right to left at 0.05 / 0.29 / 0.63 s,
  the chained right swing left to right; one at 260 HU and one 50 degrees left were never hit;
  turning 40 degrees left mid-swing hit the one at 50 degrees too. 65 per hit, once per swing.
  TF2's random melee crits still apply (a crit swing did 195 to each target); TO DECIDE.
- TF2 melee hits one target with one trace (CTFWeaponBaseMelee::DoSwingTrace). The hammer
  replaces that with an arc: every enemy (players, buildings) inside a horizontal arc in front
  of the eyes, within range, with line of sight from the eye to the target, is hit once.
  Lag compensated (like melee, CTFPlayer::WantsLagCompensationOnEntity).
- Swings alternate direction (left-to-right, then right-to-left). Knockback: a small sideways
  impulse in the swing's direction (ApplyAbsVelocityImpulse on players; none on buildings),
  tf_crusader_hammer_knockback. Only a sideways push, no pull toward or away from him.
- TF2 modifiers apply on top (random crits off for the hammer? TO DECIDE; melee has no falloff).
- Animation: see "Animations (the user's rules)" below.

Fire Strike (Phase 3)
---------------------
- A projectile fired straight from the eye, no gravity, no range limit: it flies until it hits
  the world or a prop (static props, prop_physics, func_brush-like solids). Own swept trace each
  tick, like the biotic round, with a filter that passes through players and buildings.
- Every enemy player and building it passes through takes the damage once (a hit list on the
  projectile). Teammates: passed through, no effect.
- "Shields": it goes through them. TF2's candidates are the Medic's MvM projectile shield
  (entity_medigun_shield) and, later, the Crusader's own barrier if one is added. Which shields
  exist is TO DECIDE; the rule is that nothing but world/props stops it.
- Safety: a lifetime cap (e.g. 10 s) so a projectile in an open skybox can't live forever.
- Animation: the overhead strike (see "Animations" below).

Animations (the user's rules)
-----------------------------
Goal: match the original hero's attacks for faithfulness and consistency, but above all for
readability (an enemy must tell a swing from a Fire Strike at a glance). Full name lists:
docs/demoman-animations.txt.
Reference weapon: the Eyelander (a Demoman sword; c_demo_arms cm_* set in first person), checked
by the user: its normal swings go left and right, and its crit swing is overhead. Its third-person
swing doesn't fit (the swords' overhead strike), so third person borrows the bottle's swing.
Placeholder weapon model: the Eyelander, in both views (first person and in the world model's
hand), whichever animation plays.
- Hammer swing, first person: alternate a swing to the left and a swing to the right with the
  Eyelander's normal swings (ACT_VM_HITCENTER_SPECIAL). "Left" = the blade moves toward the left.
  cm_swing_a (LEFT): winds up to the right, then the blade crosses right to left. cm_swing_b
  (RIGHT): raises the arm to the upper left, then crosses left to right. (First mapped the other
  way round from a wind-up frame; the user saw it and they were swapped, checked frame by frame.) TF2 picks between them at random, so the weapon
  plays them by name. DONE (tf_weapon_crusader_hammer, see "Alternating swing" below).
- Hammer swing, third person: the Eyelander in hand, but NOT the swords' animation. Two-handed swords (anim_slot item1,
  attackstand_ITEM1) always strike overhead. Use the bottle / stock melee swing instead
  (Melee_Swing, ACT_MP_ATTACK_STAND_MELEE, with its crouch and swim versions). It only swings
  to the left; there's no right-hand version, so both directions use it.
- Fire Strike, first person: the overhead strike, the Eyelander's crit swing
  (cm_swing_c, ACT_VM_SWINGHARD_SPECIAL).
- Fire Strike, third person: the Eyelander in hand, the swords' overhead strike (attackstand_ITEM1, ACT_MP_ATTACK_*_ITEM1).
- So the overhead is reserved for Fire Strike, in both views. If the hammer gets TF2 crits, a
  crit swing must not use cm_swing_c (it would read as a Fire Strike).
- Third-person stand/run pose: the two-handed sword pose (anim_slot item1), the user's choice.
  The bottle swing plays as a gesture over it (PLAYERANIMEVENT_CUSTOM_GESTURE).

Alternating swing (DONE, shared hero system: tf_weapon_alternating_melee.cpp/.h)
- Overwatch's rule: the first swing is to the left. The next one goes the other way only if it
  chains: it starts on the very tick the previous swing allows the next one (attack held, or a
  press buffered during the swing). Starting even one tick later is a left swing again.
- Buffer: a press during a swing queues the next one, which starts as soon as allowed even if
  the button was released. One try: if it can't swing then (stunned, ...), the buffer is spent.
- Deploying, holstering or a weapon reset forget the chain.
- Animations per side in both views: a viewmodel sequence name and third-person gestures for
  standing, crouching and swimming. Third person has its own left and right entries, so custom
  animations can show both later; for now both are the bottle's swing.
- Never the overhead crit swing for a normal swing, even on a crit.
- Tested in game (itemtest): held: LEFT RIGHT LEFT; a tap, then another after the swing was
  ready: LEFT, LEFT; a tap during that swing: RIGHT right when allowed. No prediction errors
  on the swing state (cl_showerror 2).

Charge (Phase 4)
----------------
- SHIFT starts it. The player is forced forward at the charge speed; input can steer only a
  little (a turn-rate cap, like the Targe's TF_COND_SHIELD_CHARGE turning; see
  CTFGameMovement and the "charge turn control" attribute). No jumping, no weapon use.
- Predicted movement in tf_gamemovement.cpp, state in CTFPlayerShared (networked, predicted
  end time), like Sprint.
- Ends on: the duration running out; hitting the world or a prop head-on; meeting another
  charging player (another Crusader; whether a Demoman's shield charge counts is TO DECIDE).
- Enemies met: the first enemy player is stunned and pinned: carried in front of the Crusader
  (its position set each tick by the server; its own movement is frozen by a full stun).
  Others hit along the way get knocked aside (Overwatch does this; TO CONFIRM).
- With a pinned enemy: crashing into the world or a prop deals pin damage and ends the charge;
  if the duration runs out first, the enemy is released with no pin damage (Overwatch's rule;
  TO CONFIRM the user wants the damage).
- Not pinnable: ÜberCharged players (TO DECIDE), buildings (hit and stop? TO DECIDE).
- Risks: carrying a player server-side fights its client prediction; the stun freezes its
  input, but its origin will still correct (expected to look fine, verify with cl_showerror).

Ultimate (Phase 5)
------------------
- Instant or short cast (TBD), uses the existing ultimate system (instant drain).
- A cone on the ground in front of him, out to a set range. An enemy player is stunned
  (TF2's full stun, like the Sleep Dart; duration convar) only if all hold:
  - on the ground (players in the air are not hit);
  - inside the cone and the range;
  - the wave can reach them along the floor: a trace at low height (just above a step, e.g.
    tf_crusader_ult_wave_height) from his feet to theirs is clear. Anything taller than that
    height (world, props) blocks it; low steps and slopes don't. To follow floors that go up
    and down, the check may need several traces along the path (a stepped walk), not one line.
    Start with the single low trace; refine after testing on ramps and stairs.
- ÜberCharge: probably blocks it (like the Sleep Dart). TO DECIDE.
- Effects (wave visual, sound) are placeholders.
